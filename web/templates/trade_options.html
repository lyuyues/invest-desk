Basics
① Buying calls / puts: pay a premium for the right to “buy / sell 100 shares at the strike before expiry”. The most you can lose is the premium, but time erodes value every day (theta); even if the direction is right, you can lose money if the move is not fast or big enough.
② Sell covered call: hold 100 shares and sell one call to collect premium. If the price rises above the strike, the shares are called away at the strike on expiration: upside is capped in exchange for certain income.
③ Selling cash-secured puts: reserve “Strike × 100” in cash and collect premium. If the price is below the strike at expiry, you buy 100 shares at the strike: like a discounted buy order, except you must take the shares however far it falls.
④ IV (implied volatility)High = options are expensive. IV is often high before earnings and drops sharply afterwards: buyers can lose even when right on direction (IV crush).
⑤ For contracts with wide bid-ask spreads (low open interest), prefer limit orders, placed near the mid price; market orders pay the full spread.
Only strategies that need no margin are allowed here: no naked calls / puts. Commission is 0.65 per contract. Settled automatically after the close on expiration.
